Life Insurance

Life insurance pays money to the people you choose if you pass away while the policy is active. Term life covers a set number of years, like while kids are at home or a mortgage is being paid. Permanent life is built to last your whole life.

Who it's for: Parents, spouses, homeowners, and anyone whose family counts on their income or care.

Final Expense

Final expense is a smaller whole life policy meant to help with funeral costs, final medical bills, and other end-of-life expenses. The goal is simple: so those costs don't fall on your family at an already hard time.

Who it's for: Older adults who want to take care of funeral and burial costs ahead of time.

Medicare Supplements

Medicare supplement plans (also called Medigap) work alongside Original Medicare to help pay costs it doesn't cover, like deductibles, copays, and coinsurance. Plans are labeled with letters, and the same letter offers the same basic benefits no matter which company you buy from.

Who it's for: People turning 65, or already on Original Medicare, who want more predictable medical costs.

Annuities

An annuity is a contract with an insurance company. You set money aside, and in return it can grow over time or pay you income later, sometimes for life. Terms and fees vary a lot, so we'll go over the details together before you decide anything.

Who it's for: People nearing or in retirement who want to protect savings or create a steady income.

Cancer Coverage

Cancer insurance is supplemental coverage that pays a cash benefit if you're diagnosed with a covered cancer. That money can help with what health insurance doesn't fully cover, like deductibles, travel to treatment, or bills at home while you recover.

Who it's for: Families who want an extra cushion, especially with a high-deductible health plan or a family history of cancer.

Term life covers you for a set period, often 10, 20, or 30 years. It usually costs less and is a common way to protect a family during the years they depend on your income. Whole life is a type of permanent coverage designed to last your entire life, and it can build cash value over time. It typically costs more for the same amount of coverage. Many families use one, the other, or a mix.

Final expense is a small whole life policy. When the insured person passes away, the benefit goes to the beneficiary, who can generally use it however they need. Most families use it for funeral and burial costs, final medical bills, or other debts left behind. Coverage amounts are usually smaller than a traditional life policy.

A good time to start is a few months before you turn 65. Under federal rules, your Medigap open enrollment period is the six months that begin the month you're 65 or older and enrolled in Medicare Part B. During that window, companies generally can't turn you down or charge you more because of your health. You can still apply later, but your options may be more limited.

An annuity is an agreement with an insurance company. You pay in, either all at once or over time, and the company agrees to grow that money and/or pay it back to you as income later. Some annuities can provide income for as long as you live. They often have surrender periods and fees, so it's important to understand the details before you buy.
