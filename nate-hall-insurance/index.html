Life Insurance

Life insurance pays money to the people you choose if you pass away while the policy is active. Term life covers a set number of years, like while kids are at home or a mortgage is being paid, and permanent life is built to last your whole life. Final expense coverage, a smaller policy that helps with funeral and end-of-life costs, falls here too.

Who it's for: Parents, spouses, homeowners, and anyone whose family counts on their income or care, plus older adults who want funeral costs taken care of ahead of time.

Retirement planning

Annuities & Retirement

Retirement planning is about making sure your savings last as long as you do. An annuity is a contract with an insurance company: you set money aside, and in return it can grow over time or pay you income later, sometimes for life. Terms and fees vary a lot, so we'll go over the details together before you decide anything.

Who it's for: People nearing or in retirement who want to protect what they've saved or create a steady income.

Medicare

Medicare has a lot of moving parts: Original Medicare, supplement (Medigap) plans, Medicare Advantage, and prescription drug plans. I'll help you understand how the pieces fit together and compare options so you can choose coverage that works for your doctors, prescriptions, and budget.

Who it's for: People turning 65, retiring, or already on Medicare who want a second look at their coverage.

Health Insurance

Health insurance helps pay for doctor visits, hospital stays, prescriptions, and preventive care. If you don't get coverage through an employer, I can help you compare plans and make sense of deductibles, networks, and out-of-pocket costs.

Who it's for: Self-employed folks, early retirees under 65, people between jobs, and families buying their own coverage.

Cancer & Critical Illness

This supplemental coverage pays a cash benefit if you're diagnosed with a covered condition, such as cancer, a heart attack, or a stroke, depending on the policy. That money can help with what health insurance doesn't fully cover, like deductibles, travel to treatment, or bills at home while you recover.

Who it's for: Families who want an extra cushion, especially with a high-deductible health plan or a family history of serious illness.

Long-Term Care

Long-term care coverage helps pay for care if you can no longer handle everyday activities on your own because of aging, illness, or injury. Depending on the policy, that can include in-home care, assisted living, or a nursing home. Planning ahead can help protect your savings and give your family more options.

Who it's for: Adults planning ahead who don't want future care costs to fall entirely on their savings or their kids.

Cancer insurance is supplemental coverage that pays a cash benefit if you're diagnosed with a covered cancer. That money can help with what health insurance doesn't fully cover, like deductibles, travel to treatment, or bills at home while you recover.

Who it's for: Families who want an extra cushion, especially with a high-deductible health plan or a family history of cancer.

Common Questions

General answers to get you started. Every situation is different, so I'm always happy to talk through yours.

Term life covers you for a set period, often 10, 20, or 30 years. It usually costs less and is a common way to protect a family during the years they depend on your income. Whole life is a type of permanent coverage designed to last your entire life, and it can build cash value over time. It typically costs more for the same amount of coverage. Many families use one, the other, or a mix.

Final expense is a type of life insurance: a small whole life policy. When the insured person passes away, the benefit goes to the beneficiary, who can generally use it however they need. Most families use it for funeral and burial costs, final medical bills, or other debts left behind. Coverage amounts are usually smaller than a traditional life policy.

A good time to start is a few months before you turn 65. Your Initial Enrollment Period for Medicare is a seven-month window that starts three months before the month you turn 65. If you want a Medicare supplement (Medigap) plan, your Medigap open enrollment period is the six months that begin the month you're 65 or older and enrolled in Part B. During that window, companies generally can't turn you down or charge you more because of your health. If you're still working past 65, the timing can be different, so it's worth talking through.

An annuity is an agreement with an insurance company. You pay in, either all at once or over time, and the company agrees to grow that money and/or pay it back to you as income later. Some annuities are designed to protect part of your savings from market downturns, and some can provide income for as long as you live. They often have surrender periods and fees, so it's important to understand the details before you buy.

Many people start looking in their 50s or early 60s. You're more likely to qualify while you're healthy, and costs are generally lower the younger you are when you buy. Waiting can mean higher costs or fewer options. Even if you decide it's not for you, it's worth having a plan for how care would be paid for if you ever need it.
